# Consolidated Report: Canadian Economic Outlook 2026

This structured report aggregates and compares details concerning the Canadian economy for 2026 based on reports from **Morgan Stanley, Rabobank, UBS, Deutsche Bank, Barclays,** and **Goldman Sachs**.

---

## I. Macroeconomic Projections for 2026
The sources offer a range of forecasts for Canada's growth, generally suggesting a year of moderate performance as the country adjusts to structural changes.

### Real GDP Growth
* **Rabobank:** Forecasts **0.9%** growth for 2026, a slowdown from an estimated 1.4% in 2025.
* **Deutsche Bank:** Also projects a conservative **0.9%** growth rate.
* **Morgan Stanley:** Projects **1.3%** growth, anticipating a recovery toward 1.8% in 2027.
* **UBS:** Provides the most optimistic outlook with a **1.8%** growth projection.

### Inflation (Headline CPI)
* **UBS:** Forecasts **2.1%**.
* **Morgan Stanley:** Expects **2.0%**.
* **Deutsche Bank:** Projects **2.0%** for both 2026 and 2027.
* **Rabobank:** Generally expects inflation to remain close to the central bank's target.

### Monetary Policy (Bank of Canada - BoC Rate)
* **Morgan Stanley:** Expects the BoC to remain on the sidelines through 2026, ending the year at **2.25%** before a single hike in early 2027.
* **Goldman Sachs:** Projects the policy rate will end 2026 at approximately **3.0%**.
* **Rabobank:** Notes that Canada was among the 9 out of 10 G10 countries that cut rates in 2025.

---

## II. Structural Drivers: Labor and Immigration
**Morgan Stanley** provides a deep dive into the unique structural adjustments facing Canada during this period:

* **Immigration Caps:** The government has imposed limits on inflows for 2025–2027. This is expected to significantly slow labor supply growth and reduce the "breakeven" payroll requirement.
* **Unemployment:** Despite softening labor demand, the rate is expected to remain relatively stable due to slower population growth. Forecast: **6.9%**.
* **Shelter Inflation:** The reduction in population growth is expected to moderate shelter inflation by easing housing demand.

---

## III. Trade and Geopolitics
Canada’s economic outlook is heavily influenced by its relationship with the United States and Washington's shifting "Grand Macro Strategy."

* **USMCA Review:** A formal review is scheduled for **July 1, 2026**. This is a major source of uncertainty that may keep business investment muted.
* **Trade Pressures:** Rabobank notes Washington is leveraging the USMCA to pressure Canada to align with the US anti-China agenda.
* **Tariff Exposure:** Businesses are adjusting to higher production costs from US trade policies. However, Canada is a partner in US efforts to diversify critical mineral supply chains away from China.
* **Energy Exports:** Canada is listed as one of the largest sources of non-OPEC+ oil supply growth for 2026.

---

## IV. Fiscal and Investment Outlook

### Budgetary Position
* **Deutsche Bank:** Projects a **2.3%** budget deficit (as % of GDP), with gross government debt reaching **43.4%**.
* **Morgan Stanley:** Forecasts a **2.0%** deficit, but sees federal debt rising to **73.6%** of GDP.

### Other Indicators
* **R&D Spending:** Rabobank data shows spending is roughly **1.7% to 1.8%** of GDP (middle of the pack globally).
* **Regulatory Status:** Goldman Sachs Asset Management notes it is registered as a portfolio manager in all provinces.
